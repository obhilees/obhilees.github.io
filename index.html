Hi!

I am a graduate student in speech-language pathology at the University of Kansas. I am interested in the mechanisms underlying motor planning and speech production. I am particularly interested in how studying atypical speech can provide insight into typical speech processes, many of which remain not fully understood. My academic focus lies in acoustic phonetics and the ways in which fine-grained acoustic features reflect underlying linguistic and motor processes.

I am especially interested in how people use speech, language, voice, and other forms of communication in everyday life. I am building this website as a space to share my academic work, research interests, projects, and professional development.

My current interests include speech-language assessment, child language development, multilingual communication, clinical documentation, and accessible communication.

This site will eventually include selected coursework, research summaries, clinical reflections, lab reports, and other projects related to speech-language pathology.
