Hi!

I am a graduate student in speech-language pathology at the University of Kansas. I am interested in the mechanisms underlying motor planning and speech production. I am particularly interested in how studying atypical speech can provide insight into typical speech processes, many of which remain not fully understood. My academic focus lies in acoustic phonetics and the ways in which fine-grained acoustic features reflect underlying linguistic and motor processes.

Clinically, I am committed to improving the early detection of neurodegenerative diseases through speech. I am interested in developing detailed acoustic profiles that could support diagnosis by capturing subtle changes in voice, articulation, and timing. My goal is to contribute to a future where speech serves as a reliable, non-invasive marker of neurological health.

During my master's in Jadavpur University, I designed a Divided Visual Field (DVF) paradigm experiment to study the effects of residual motor planning. I was curious about how motoric speech planning is inhibited in a short window of time and I was keen on testing the DVF paradigm’s compatibility as an inexpensive and non-invasive method of studying lateralization of speech functions in the brain.

This site will eventually include selected coursework, research summaries, clinical reflections, lab reports, and other projects related to speech-language pathology.
